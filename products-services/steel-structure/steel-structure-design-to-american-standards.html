Steel structure design to American standards

The AISC, ASCE and other American codes behind structural steel design, and how they are applied.

Designing a steel structure to American standards means checking its loads, members, connections, materials, welds and bolts against a linked set of United States codes and standards. Most of them come from the American Institute of Steel Construction (AISC), the American Society of Civil Engineers (ASCE), the American Welding Society (AWS) and ASTM International. The International Building Code (IBC) brings them together by adopting them by reference.

Why American standards

The AISC Specification for Structural Steel Buildings (ANSI/AISC 360) is an American National Standard, and the International Building Code adopts it by reference. AISC publishes it, together with its seismic provisions (AISC 341) and its Code of Standard Practice (AISC 303), as free downloads.

These standards are also used outside the United States. The steel chapter of the National Structural Code of the Philippines (NSCP) is based on AISC specifications. Owners of foreign-invested factories and warehouses often name AISC, AWS and ASTM standards in their project specifications, so that their consultants and inspectors can review the design against a framework they know.

Two design methods: LRFD and ASD

AISC 360 sets out two ways of checking strength in one unified document. Both start from the same nominal strength, Rn, of each member or connection. They differ in where the safety margin is applied.

  • Load and Resistance Factor Design (LRFD): the loads are multiplied by load factors, and the required strength must not exceed the design strength φRn, where φ is a resistance factor of less than 1.0.
  • Allowable Strength Design (ASD): the loads are combined at service level, and the required strength must not exceed the allowable strength Rn/Ω, where Ω is a safety factor greater than 1.0.

The load combinations for each method come from ASCE 7.

How a design comes together

  1. Design criteria. The building code, the standards and their editions, the building's use, the site conditions and the owner's requirements are agreed and recorded first.
  2. Loads. Dead, live, wind, seismic and other environmental loads are found from ASCE 7. Equipment loads, such as overhead cranes, come from the project requirements. The loads are then combined using the LRFD or ASD load combinations.
  3. Analysis and stability. The frame is analyzed with a structural model. AISC 360 requires the design to account for stability, including second-order effects, initial imperfections and the reduction in stiffness as steel yields. Its direct analysis method is one way to do this.
  4. Member design. Columns, rafters, beams and bracing are checked for tension, compression, bending, shear and combined forces.
  5. Connections. Bolted and welded connections are designed to AISC 360, with bolting to the RCSC Specification and welding to AWS D1.1. In seismic force-resisting systems, AISC 341 and AISC 358 add requirements.
  6. Serviceability. Deflection, drift and vibration are checked so that the building works well in everyday use, not only at its strength limits.
  7. Drawings. Design, shop and erection drawings are prepared. AISC 303 sets out the usual responsibilities and the tolerances for fabrication and erection.
  8. Quality control. Material test certificates, welding procedures, welder qualifications and inspections of welds and bolted joints are recorded, following the quality requirements of AISC 360 and AWS D1.1.

Common materials

  • ASTM A992: the preferred specification for wide-flange (W) shapes. Its minimum yield strength is 50 ksi (about 345 MPa), and its minimum tensile strength is 65 ksi (about 450 MPa).
  • ASTM A572 Grade 50: high-strength low-alloy steel for plates and shapes, with a minimum yield strength of 50 ksi (about 345 MPa).
  • ASTM A36: carbon steel for plates, angles and other shapes, with a minimum yield strength of 36 ksi (about 250 MPa).
  • ASTM A500: cold-formed hollow structural sections (HSS).
  • ASTM F3125: high-strength structural bolts. It includes Grades A325 and A490 and replaced the separate A325 and A490 standards.

Pre-engineered steel buildings

In a pre-engineered steel building, the main frames are designed to AISC 360, and the cold-formed purlins and girts to AISI S100. The MBMA Metal Building Systems Manual adds load and design guidance written for this type of building. Its 2024 edition conforms to the 2024 IBC and ASCE 7-22.
